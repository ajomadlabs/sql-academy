# Job Search

**Applications begin Day 25.** Ramp through Day 40, then sustained volume until she signs.

---

## Why Day 25 and not Day 5

Urgency argues for applying immediately. I'd still wait, for one reason: **the first interview at any given company is usually the only one for 6–12 months.** Applying with shaky fundamentals spends those chances on rounds she'll fail. Three weeks of preparation converts dramatically better, and the pipeline she builds from Day 25 will still produce interviews inside the 60-day window.

The exception: if an unusually good role appears in Weeks 1–3, apply anyway. Don't pass on a genuinely strong opportunity to protect a schedule.

## Volume targets

| Days | Applications/day | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 25–32 | 3–5 | Calibration. Mid-tier companies. Learn what the JDs demand. |
| 33–45 | 8–10 | Full volume, all tiers. |
| 46–60 | 5–8 | Sustained, plus energy diverted into live interview processes. |

**Roughly 250–350 applications total.** That number surprises people. Normal conversion is 5–10% to a first-round, and a handful of offers from that. This is a volume game with a quality filter, not a precision exercise — treat a low response rate as arithmetic, not a verdict.

## Where to apply

**Tier 1 — Services companies (primary target, ~50% of volume)**
Infosys, TCS, Cognizant, Accenture, LTIMindtree, Wipro, HCL, Capgemini, Tech Mahindra, Mphasis, Hexaware, Zensar.

Strategically the right primary target, and not a step down: interview loops are predictable and SQL-centric, 5 years of tenure is genuinely valued, they hire in volume year-round, and lateral moves often come with a real band increase. A move here buys the runway to do the Data Engineering pivot properly next year.

**Tier 2 — GCCs / captive centres (~30%)**
Banking and financial services (JPMC, Goldman, Wells Fargo, Deutsche, Standard Chartered), retail and healthcare captives, insurance. Heavy SQL usage, better pay than services, more stable than startups, interviews are demanding but fair and well-scoped.

**Tier 3 — Product companies and startups (~20%)**
Higher pay and higher bar. Worth applying to for the interview practice even where the odds are long — a failed product-company loop in week 5 is genuinely useful preparation.

## Channels, ranked by actual return

1. **Naukri** — dominant for the Indian market. Update the profile *weekly*; recruiters filter by last-active date, and this alone materially changes inbound volume.
2. **LinkedIn** — apply, but the bigger value is recruiter inbound and direct outreach.
3. **Direct on company career pages** — better signal than aggregators, worth the extra minutes for Tier 2.
4. **Referrals** — by far the highest conversion. See below.
5. **Recruitment consultancies** — they work volume for you; register with 3–5.
6. **instahyre / Cutshort / Hirist** — decent for product roles.

## Referrals — the highest-leverage channel

A referral converts several times better than a cold application. Five years of colleagues is the single most underused asset here.

- List every ex-colleague who has moved on to another company. LinkedIn makes this easy.
- Message them directly. Short, specific, no apology: *"Hi — I'm looking at SQL/database developer roles and saw you're at X. Would you be open to referring me? Happy to send my resume."*
- Most people say yes. Asking feels far more awkward than it is, and referrals are usually rewarded at the referrer's end anyway.
- Target 20–30 referral requests across the plan.

## Search keywords

`SQL Developer` · `Database Developer` · `PL/SQL Developer` · `T-SQL Developer` · `Backend Developer SQL` · `Data Analyst` · `Database Engineer` · `ETL Developer` · `MSBI Developer`

Filter to **3–7 years** experience. Don't apply below 3 — it signals underconfidence and usually pays worse.

## Reading a job description

Requirements lists are wish lists, not thresholds. **Apply at ~60% match.** If she waits for 100%, she'll apply to almost nothing — this pattern is well documented and disproportionately affects candidates who are already underconfident, which is precisely the situation here.

Hard filters (skip these): 8+ years required; a completely different core stack (Java-heavy when she isn't); explicit "immediate joiner only" if her notice is long.

Soft (apply anyway): "preferred" qualifications; cloud tools she hasn't used; a degree mismatch; slightly higher experience band.

## Tracking

Keep a spreadsheet — company, role, date applied, channel, contact, status, next action, notes. Without it, follow-ups get missed, and follow-ups convert.

**Follow up once, 7–10 days after applying**, via LinkedIn to a recruiter or hiring manager where possible. Once only. Then let it go.

## Notice period

If it's 90 days, put it on the application honestly and early. Discovering it at offer stage wastes everyone's time and occasionally kills an otherwise-done deal. Some companies will filter her out — that's fine, and better found on day one. Many negotiate, and buy-outs are common.

## Managing the emotional load

The response rate will be low. That's structural — it reflects volume and market conditions, not her ability, and it will still feel personal, particularly to someone already short on confidence.

Two protections worth building in:
- **Batch applications into the fixed evening hour.** Don't let them leak into study time or into the rest of the day.
- **Track applications sent, not replies received.** Sent is the part she controls; replies aren't. Measuring the uncontrollable metric is how people quit at week five.
